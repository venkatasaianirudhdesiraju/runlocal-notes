## Introduction

Self-hosting AI models at home requires more than just a good internet connection and some code. You'll need suitable hardware to handle the computational demands of even relatively small models. In this post, we'll outline the practical requirements for running 7B-13B parameter local models comfortably at home, without going overboard.

## What's the Bare Minimum?

To get started, you'll need a machine with:

* 16 GB of RAM (32 GB or more recommended for larger models)
* A dedicated graphics card (NVIDIA or AMD) with at least 8 GB of VRAM (16 GB or more recommended)
* A multi-core CPU (at least 4 cores, but 8 or more is ideal)
* A decent power supply unit (PSU) to ensure stable operation
* A suitable motherboard that supports your chosen CPU and GPU

## Choosing the Right CPU and GPU

When it comes to CPUs, the type of model you plan to run will greatly influence your choice. For smaller models (7B-10B parameters), a mid-range CPU from the past 2-3 years (e.g., AMD Ryzen 5 or Intel Core i5) should suffice. For larger models (10B-13B parameters), consider a high-end CPU from the past year or two (e.g., AMD Ryzen 9 or Intel Core i9).

The graphics card is where most AI enthusiasts go overboard. For smaller models, an entry-level to mid-range GPU (e.g., NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1660 or AMD Radeon RX 5600 XT) will be more than sufficient. For larger models, a mid-range to high-end GPU (e.g., NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 or AMD Radeon RX 6700 XT) is necessary.

## Mini PC vs Tower: Which is Best?

Both mini PCs and towers have their pros and cons. Mini PCs are great for space-constrained environments, but they often lack upgradability and may not be as future-proof. Towers, on the other hand, offer better upgradability and more room for expansion, but they can take up valuable desk space.

For AI workloads, a tower is generally a better choice due to the ease of upgrading and adding more hardware as needed. However, if you're short on space, a mini PC with a strong GPU and decent CPU can still provide excellent performance.

## Additional Considerations

When building your AI rig, consider the following:

* **Cooling**: Ensure your machine has adequate cooling to prevent overheating, especially during long training sessions.
* **Power consumption**: Choose a machine with a suitable PSU to avoid power supply constraints.
* **Noise**: If you plan to run your machine in a home office or living room, consider a machine with a relatively quiet fan or opt for a fanless solution.
* **Future-proofing**: Plan for future model sizes and performance demands by selecting hardware that can be easily upgraded or replaced.

## Conclusion

Self-hosting AI models at home requires careful consideration of hardware requirements. By understanding the bare minimum and choosing suitable components, you can build a machine that can handle 7B-13B parameter local models comfortably without breaking the bank. Remember to prioritize upgradability, cooling, and power consumption to ensure your machine runs smoothly and efficiently.
